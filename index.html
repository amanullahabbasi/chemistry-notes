Quantitative Toolkit

Mole definition n = m / M
Solution moles n = cV
Gas at r.t.p. n = V / 24
Average atomic mass Ar = Σ(isotope mass × % abundance) / 100
Percentage yield actual yield / theoretical yield × 100%
Atom economy Mr of desired product / total Mr of products × 100%

Units to keep straight

  • 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³
  • V in n = cV must be in dm³
  • r.t.p. molar gas volume = 24 dm³ mol⁻¹
  • Avogadro constant = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

Exam reminder

Use the balanced equation first. Mole ratio always comes from the coefficients, not from the raw formula.

Particles, States and Separation

PropertySolidLiquidGas
ArrangementClosely packed, regularClose, randomFar apart, random
MovementVibrate in positionSlide past each otherRapid random motion
Shape and volumeFixedFixed volumeNeither fixed
CompressibilityVery lowVery lowHigh

State changes

Melting, boiling, and sublimation absorb energy. Freezing and condensation release energy.

Diffusion

Net movement from higher concentration to lower concentration. Faster at higher temperature and for lighter particles.

Separation methods

  • Filtration: insoluble solid from liquid
  • Crystallisation: soluble solid from solution
  • Simple distillation: solvent or large boiling-point difference
  • Fractional distillation: miscible liquids with closer boiling points
  • Chromatography: soluble substances in mixtures
Chromatography Rf = distance moved by substance / distance moved by solvent

Atomic Structure and the Periodic Table

Subatomic particles

Proton: charge +1, relative mass 1
Neutron: charge 0, relative mass 1
Electron: charge -1, relative mass 1/1840
Core relations atomic number = protons
mass number = protons + neutrons
neutrons = mass number - atomic number

Isotopes

Same proton number, different neutron number. Same chemical properties, different masses.

Periods

Period number gives the number of occupied electron shells.

Groups

Group number for Groups 1 to 7 gives the number of outer-shell electrons.

Noble gases

Full outer shell, very unreactive, exist as single atoms.
AreaTrend
Down Group 1Reactivity increases because outer electron is lost more easily
Down Group 7Reactivity decreases because gain of one electron becomes harder
Across a periodMetallic character decreases and valence electrons increase
Transition metalsHigh density, high melting points, colored compounds, variable oxidation states, catalytic activity

Bonding and Structure

Ionic bonding

Electron transfer between metal and non-metal. Strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

Covalent bonding

Shared pair of electrons between non-metals.

Metallic bonding

Positive metal ions in a sea of delocalised electrons.
Structure typeTypical propertiesExamples
Ionic latticeHigh melting point; conducts when molten or aqueous; brittleNaCl, MgO
Simple molecularLow melting point; poor conductorCO₂, CH₄, H₂O
Giant covalentVery high melting point; usually poor conductorDiamond, SiO₂
Metallic latticeGood conductor; malleable; ductileCu, Al, Fe

Graphite

Each carbon bonds to three others. One delocalised electron per atom, so it conducts electricity. Layers slide, so it is soft.

Diamond

Each carbon bonds to four others. Very hard, very high melting point, does not conduct.

Formulae, Equations and Moles

Empirical formula steps

Convert masses to moles, divide by the smallest value, then scale to whole numbers if needed.

Molecular formula

Find empirical formula mass first, then use:
multiplier = molecular mass / empirical formula mass
Concentration c = n / V
Titration relation c₁V₁ / n₁ = c₂V₂ / n₂
Percentage purity mass of pure substance / total mass × 100%
Conservation rule: total mass and total charge must balance. Never change subscripts to balance an equation. Only change coefficients.
06

Acids, Bases and Salts

Definitions

Acid produces H⁺ in water. Base neutralises acid. Alkali is a soluble base producing OH⁻.

pH ideas

Low pH is acidic, 7 is neutral, high pH is alkaline.

Oxides

Metal oxides are usually basic. Non-metal oxides are usually acidic. Some, like Al₂O₃ and ZnO, are amphoteric.
Reaction patternProducts
Acid + metalSalt + hydrogen
Acid + baseSalt + water
Acid + carbonateSalt + water + carbon dioxide
Acid + ammoniaAmmonium salt

Salt names from acids

Hydrochloric acid gives chlorides, nitric acid gives nitrates, sulfuric acid gives sulfates, ethanoic acid gives ethanoates.

Preparation methods

Titration for soluble salts from alkalis. Add excess insoluble base or metal, then filter and crystallise, for many other soluble salts.

Metals, Reactivity and Extraction

Reactivity series: K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb, H, Cu, Ag, Au

Displacement

A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its compound.

Extraction rule

Metals above carbon are usually extracted by electrolysis. Metals below carbon can often be extracted by reduction with carbon or carbon monoxide.
ReactionTrend
Metal + waterVery reactive metals form hydroxide + hydrogen
Metal + steamModerately reactive metals form oxide + hydrogen

Alloys

Harder than pure metals because different-sized atoms disturb the layers and make sliding harder.
09

Rates and Energetics

Collision theory

Reactions happen when particles collide with enough energy and the right orientation.
Rate expression rate = change in quantity / time

Increase rate

Higher temperature, higher concentration, higher gas pressure, greater surface area, or a catalyst.

Exothermic

Releases energy to surroundings. Temperature rises. ΔH is negative.

Endothermic

Takes in energy from surroundings. Temperature falls. ΔH is positive.
Calorimetry Q = mcΔT
Enthalpy from heating data ΔH = -Q / n

Organic Chemistry

Homologous seriesGeneral formulaKey feature
AlkanesCₙH₂ₙ₊₂Saturated, single bonds
AlkenesCₙH₂ₙUnsaturated, one double bond
AlcoholsCₙH₂ₙ₊₁OHContain -OH
Carboxylic acidsCₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOHContain -COOH

Combustion

Complete combustion makes carbon dioxide and water. Incomplete combustion can make carbon monoxide or soot.

Alkenes test

Bromine water turns from orange to colorless with an alkene.

Cracking

Long-chain hydrocarbons break into shorter alkanes and alkenes using heat and a catalyst.

Important reactions

Addition across a double bond, hydration of ethene to ethanol, fermentation of glucose to ethanol and carbon dioxide, esterification of alcohols with carboxylic acids.

Polymer basics

Addition polymers form from many alkene molecules joining. The repeating unit comes from opening the double bond.

Air, Water and Environment

Air composition

About 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, around 1% noble gases and carbon dioxide.

Water treatment

Filtration removes solids, chlorination kills microorganisms, distillation gives pure water.

Hard water

Contains dissolved calcium or magnesium ions. Temporary hardness is removed by boiling.
PollutantMain sourceMain effect
COIncomplete combustionPoisonous, reduces oxygen transport in blood
SO₂Burning sulfur impurities in fuelsAcid rain, respiratory irritation
NOₓHigh-temperature engine reactionsAcid rain, photochemical smog
CO₂Combustion and respirationEnhanced greenhouse effect

Rusting control

Painting, oiling, plastic coating, galvanising, or sacrificial protection.

Fertiliser point

Plants need nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium for healthy growth.
12

Industrial Chemistry and Materials

Haber process

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃
Conditions: iron catalyst, about 450°C, about 200 atm. Lower temperature and higher pressure favor ammonia, but compromises are used for speed and cost.

Contact process

2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃
Catalyst: vanadium(V) oxide. Sulfuric acid is made from sulfur trioxide.

Limestone cycle

CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂
Ca(OH)₂ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + H₂O

Ceramics, glasses and polymers

Ceramics are hard and heat resistant but brittle. Glasses are inert and transparent. Plastics are useful because they are light, moldable, and often resistant to corrosion.

Qualitative Analysis and Memory Lists

Gas tests

  • Hydrogen: squeaky pop with lighted splint
  • Oxygen: relights glowing splint
  • Carbon dioxide: turns limewater milky
  • Chlorine: bleaches damp litmus paper
  • Ammonia: turns damp red litmus blue

Flame tests

Lithium crimson, sodium yellow, potassium lilac, calcium orange-red, copper blue-green.

Solubility rules

All nitrates are soluble. All sodium, potassium, and ammonium salts are soluble.

Mostly insoluble

Most carbonates and hydroxides are insoluble except sodium, potassium, and ammonium compounds.

Common exceptions

Silver chloride, lead(II) chloride, barium sulfate, and lead(II) sulfate are important insoluble examples.
